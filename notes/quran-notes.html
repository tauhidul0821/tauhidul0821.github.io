Al-Fath (48:10)

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُبَايِعُونَكَ إِنَّمَا يُبَايِعُونَ اللَّهَ يَدُ اللَّهِ فَوْقَ أَيْدِيهِمْ ۚ فَمَنْ نَكَثَ فَإِنَّمَا يَنْكُثُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ۖ وَمَنْ أَوْفَىٰ بِمَا عَاهَدَ عَلَيْهُ اللَّهَ فَسَيُؤْتِيهِ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا

Transliteration: Inna allatheena yubayiAAoonaka innama yubayiAAoona Allaha yadu Allahi fawqa aydeehim faman nakatha fainnama yankuthu AAala nafsihi waman awfa bima AAahada AAalayhu Allaha fasayuteehi ajran AAatheeman

Translation: "Verily those who plight their fealty to thee do no less than plight their fealty to Allah: the Hand of Allah is over their hands: then any one who violates his oath, does so to the harm of his own soul, and any one who fulfils what he has covenanted with Allah,- Allah will soon grant him a great Reward."

Meaning of the Verse

Surah Al-Fath (48:10) is deeply tied to a pivotal moment in Islamic history known as the Pledge of the Tree (Bay'at al-Ridwan) at Hudaibiyah. It establishes the spiritual and legal weight of pledging allegiance to Prophet Muhammad, acting as a profound statement on loyalty, divine support, and the sanctity of promises.

Historical Context

In 628 CE (6 AH), the Prophet and his companions traveled toward Mecca to perform the pilgrimage but were intercepted by the Quraysh. When a rumor spread that their peaceful emissary, Uthman ibn Affan, had been assassinated in Mecca, the companions gathered under a tree. They placed their hands on the Prophet's hand, swearing a solemn oath to stand by him to the death and not flee.

Meaning of Key Phrases

  • Equating the Pledge: "Verily those who plight their fealty to thee do no less than plight their fealty to Allah." This establishes a foundational Islamic principle: obeying the Messenger is indistinguishable from obeying God. Because the Prophet acted solely as Allah's representative, committing to his leadership was a direct commitment to the Creator.
  • Divine Endorsement: "The Hand of Allah is over their hands." During the physical act of the pledge, where hands were stacked upon one another, this powerful metaphor signifies that Allah's power, blessing, and witness encompassed them. It vividly illustrates that they were not just making a political or military alliance, but a sacred covenant backed by divine authority.
  • Personal Responsibility: "Then any one who violates his oath, does so to the harm of his own soul." Breaking this covenant does not diminish God's power or harm the Prophet; the spiritual and moral ruin falls entirely on the oath-breaker.
  • The Ultimate Promise: "And any one who fulfils what he has covenanted with Allah,- Allah will soon grant him a great Reward." For the companions who stood firm, the immediate reward was the peaceful, strategic victory of the Treaty of Hudaibiyah, while the ultimate reward promised is Paradise.

Scholars of Tafsir (Quranic exegesis) emphasize that while this verse addresses a specific historical event, its core principle is timeless. The imagery offers profound reassurance that when believers align their actions with divine guidance and honor their commitments, they are supported by a power far greater than themselves.
