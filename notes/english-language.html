3. Subject-Verb Agreement

Plural forms: gentry, people.

Basic Rule: Nouns joined by "and"

If two nouns are connected using and, the subject becomes plural, so the verb must be plural.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Jamal and Kamal is/are going.areJamal and Kamal together are plural.

Exceptions (idiomatic pairs)

Some noun pairs joined by and are treated as a single idea, so they stay singular even though there appear to be two nouns.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Horse and carriage is/are ready.isTreated as one unit, so singular.
Time and tide waits/wait for none.waitsLooks plural, but it's an idiomatic singular subject.
Bread and butter is/are my favourite.isOne combined idea (a single dish/idea), singular.

Singular verbs to remember: is, was, has, does.

Correlative Conjunctions

With or / nor / but joining two nouns, the verb agrees with the noun closest to it — not with the whole subject.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Shima or her friends have/has joined in the party.have"her friends" (plural) is closest to the verb.

Common correlative pairs: either … or, neither … nor.

Indefinite Pronoun Subjects

Sentences starting with each, every, everybody, everyone, everything, somebody, someone, something, nobody, no one, nothing, anybody, anyone, anything are singular, so the verb must be singular.

ExampleAnswer
Every mother love/loves her child.loves

"Of" Phrases

The verb agrees with the head word of the subject, not the noun after "of".

ExampleAnswerWhy
A selection of Kazi Nazrul's poems is/are my favourite.isHead word "A selection" is singular.
Some of my students have/has passed in the exam.have"students" is plural here, so plural verb.

Remember, phrases like one of, some of, either of, neither of, none of usually take a singular verb — check the head word case by case.

Uncountable Nouns

If the subject is an uncountable noun (oil, water, a lot of money, furniture), the verb should be singular.

ExampleAnswer
A lot of money have/has been spent.has

"With / Along With" Phrases

Rule: with, along with, together with, no less than, in addition to — when two subjects are joined this way, the verb agrees with the main subject only, not the added phrase.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Father with his three sons have/has been invited in the party.has"Father" is the main subject, and it's singular.

Fractions

If the fraction's value is more than one and the noun is countable, the subject is plural, so the verb should be plural. If the noun is uncountable, the verb stays singular regardless of the fraction.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Two thirds of the students have/has passed in the exam.have"students" is countable and plural.
Two third of the work have/has been done.has"work" is uncountable, so singular verb.

Definite Quantity as Subject

If the subject is formed by a definite/specific quantity adjective (a pair of, a specific number), the verb is singular.

ExampleAnswerWhy
A pair of shoes has/have been bought for me.has"A pair" is singular.
Ten miles is/are a long distance.isA definite number before the noun makes the verb singular.
Many a man have/has tried to complete the job.has"Many a man" = singular ("a man", not "men").

Gerund & Infinitive Subjects

If the subject is a gerund (verb + ing) or an infinitive (to + v1), it is treated as singular, so the verb should be singular.

ExampleAnswerWhy
Telling lies is/are a great sin.is"Telling" is a gerund subject, singular.
To walk is/are good for health.is"To walk" is an infinitive subject, singular.
